Why this exists

Out-of-home advertising runs on estimated impressions. A bulletin does not know who saw it. Sites go dark, posters get damaged, digital screens have downtime, and agencies typically discover all of it by hand at end of campaign, during reconciliation.

I watched that reconciliation happen for five months from inside the agency, then built this. It ranks under-delivering inventory while the flight is still running: every placement is measured against its contracted impressions prorated to the days elapsed, so a live campaign is judged on the days that have actually run rather than penalised for the ones that have not happened yet. The number that matters is spend at risk: billed money attached to placements that are behind what was contracted.

05

How to use this

four steps, about a minute
  1. Read the KPI strip. Two amber cards lead: how many placements breached the flag line, and what that shortfall is worth in billed spend. The rest give spend billed to date, impressions delivered against contracted to date, and the discount achieved off rate card. Everything is measured to the as-of date above the strip.
  2. Open panel 01. Every placement more than 5% behind contract for the days that have run, worst first. Rows marked LIVE are still in the air — those are the ones worth a phone call, because there is still flight left to make the shortfall up in.
  3. Filter to a client or city. The table and the count re-filter live. Pick a client and you have the reconciliation conversation for that account, ready to have.
  4. Check panel 03 before renewing. Cost per thousand by format, on verified rather than contracted delivery. This is which inventory type is actually efficient once you account for what really ran — read within a format, not across, for the reason under the chart.
06

How it works

01 / DATA

Normalized data model

Four tables, sites and campaigns and placements and delivery, with enforced foreign keys. A placement joins a campaign to a site over a date window; delivery records what it achieved each day.

02 / METRICS

Tested metrics layer

Prorated delivery variance, spend to date, CPM, rate efficiency, daily showing level and a reach estimate. Each function does one thing and is unit-tested against inputs small enough to check by hand — including the mid-flight case, where a healthy placement must not flag.

03 / SQL

Analytical SQL

CTEs and window functions. ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY …) to rank the worst sites inside each campaign, LAG() for month-over-month change per client.
